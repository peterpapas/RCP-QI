# Proof complexity of certified branch elimination

## Systems and what a discarded partition establishes

A discarded prefix `p` asserts `F∧p` is unsatisfiable. A certificate must show **all completions** of `p` fail, not merely that a sampled branch was unmarked. In the restricted model studied here, every leaf names an *original* CNF clause all of whose literals are falsified by that leaf prefix. Both children of every binary branch are present. A leaf is checked by scanning the clause and prefix; a complete tree is checked bottom-up. No learned clauses, arbitrary SAT-oracle verdicts, global algebraic inferences, postselection or quantum computation are permitted. Lean's `checkClauseConflict_correct`, `conflictTree_sound` and `conflictTree_root_unsat` establish the finite-tree certificate semantics. They do not construct a resolution derivation.

| Proof system | Certificate and comparison to this tree |
| --- | --- |
| Resolution | Derives clauses by resolving opposite literals; tree-like resolution uses each derived node along one path. The restricted conflict tree has a direct simulation with weakening below. |
| Regular resolution | No variable is resolved twice on any proof path. A decision tree branching on each variable at most once may yield a regular refutation under the translation; the Lean tree itself does **not** enforce this restriction. |
| Polynomial calculus / Nullstellensatz | Polynomial identities refute a Boolean system; degree/size lower bounds are system-specific. No simulation from an arbitrary algebraic or quantum partition oracle to the conflict tree is shown. [Beame et al.](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~beame/papers/focsstell.pdf) supply original Nullstellensatz lower-bound work. |
| Cutting planes | Linear-inequality derivations with integer rounding; can express inferences beyond clause resolution. See [Chvátal's original account](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~chvatal/Bonn84.pdf). |
| Sum of squares | Algebraic nonnegativity certificate, with degree and coefficient/bit complexity; no unrestricted simulation by the tree. See [Grigoriev's original lower-bound work](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~grigorev/pub/square_journal.pdf). |

## T3 analytic translation into tree-like resolution with weakening

For a prefix `p`, let `D(p)` be the clause of literals opposite to its assignments. At a leaf, the cited input clause `C` is falsified by `p`, so `C⊆D(p)`; weaken `C` to `D(p)`. At a node branching on `x_i`, the false child derives `D(p)∨x_i`, the true child derives `D(p)∨¬x_i`, and one resolution step derives `D(p)`. At the empty root, `D(∅)` is the empty clause. Induction on the tree proves the translation. With `T` tree nodes and maximum depth `d`, the proof has `O(Td)` literal-addition/weakening work and `O(T)` resolution steps; checking leaf conflicts naively costs at most `O(T·c·d)` for maximum leaf-clause length `c`. If each variable is branched on at most once per path, `d≤n`. These are **analytic** bounds, not Lean-verified translation code.

Weakening does not invalidate the lower-bound comparison: replace each derived clause by a subclause derivable without weakening. At a weakening step keep the parent subclause; at a resolution step, resolve the two subclauses if both retain the pivot, or keep a child subclause that omits it. The resulting clause remains a subclause of the intended resolvent. The empty root remains empty, with no larger tree. This is the established DPLL/tree-like-resolution correspondence, not a newly discovered proof system; [Beame–Kautz–Sabharwal](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~sabhar/jobapps/pubs/sabharwal-pub-cl-jair04.pdf) discuss the equivalence, and [Ben-Sasson–Wigderson](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf) analyze proof width and size. [Haken's pigeonhole lower bound](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2885%2990144-6) therefore constrains this restricted exhaustive conflict-tree architecture on the corresponding unsatisfiable CNFs, after the simulation overhead. It does **not** constrain an opaque polynomial-time SAT-existence subroutine, clause learning/CDCL, a stronger algebraic proof system, or an arbitrary quantum circuit. Pigeonhole instances violate the unique-solution promise, so this corollary is about branch-elimination architecture, not the V1 unique-witness setting.

The project has no proof that a bounded-error quantum partition decision always yields a short *classical checkable* certificate. If its discard verdict is wrong, the tree is unsound; if it is correct but opaque, the translation above is inapplicable. That is the decisive transfer blocker for T4.
