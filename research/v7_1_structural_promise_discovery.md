# V7.1 structural promise discovery

**Status:** mathematical and original-source review, 26 November 2026. No new quantum circuit, statevector experiment, or V8 approval. The V1–V6 and V5.1 raw experiments remain unchanged. The V5.1 lack of an observed direct-CDCL crossover is negative evidence for its tested classical recursive architecture under two seeds and two-second method budgets, not a theorem about all structured algorithms.

## Scope and taxonomy

A *language* such as ordinary 3-SAT contains all syntactically valid inputs. A *restricted family* has an explicitly defined representation and membership rule. A *promise problem* requires an input relation that need not hold for arbitrary inputs; the algorithm's behavior outside the promise may be unspecified. An *oracle problem* may give coherent access to functions that an ordinary CNF does not supply. A quantum query separation in an oracle model does not by itself give a time separation on explicit SAT instances.

| Promise source | Example | Access and certification obligation | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supplied algebraic oracle | Simon collision function or hidden shift pair | State who constructs each reversible oracle and count its gates, queries and output register | A Boolean SAT verifier is not the richer oracle; construction may encode the answer |
| Syntactically recognizable input | XOR equations, 2-CNF, explicit affine `Ax=b`, certified variable permutation | Parse and certify syntax; compile reversible maps; include classical specialized algorithms | The family may be polynomial-time classically |
| Supplied structural certificate | Tree decomposition, group generators, affine parameterization | Verify certificate and bound representative/preparation/update cost | Certificate generation or use can dominate; width may be large |
| Semantic relationship | `f(Tx)=f(x)` or `1−f(x)` for all `x` | Prove the universal property from the input or charge an oracle that guarantees it | Even a supplied bit-flip invariance test can encode UNSAT (V7 Lean reduction) |
| Statistical promise | A computable branch statistic has a guaranteed sign and gap | Specify distribution, independent certificate or assumption, error and sample/query budget | A heuristic correlation on selected data does not certify the correct branch |

For each candidate below, `P` denotes preprocessing/discovery/certification, `S` coherent preparation, `U` update or transformation, `C` verifier/marking, `R` inverse/reflection, `M` measurement, and `V` final verification. A complete quantum cost has the form `P + Σ_attempts[S + Q_U U + Q_C C + Q_R R + M + V]`, including failed attempts and any new preparation each time, with logical queries, compiled gates, depth, ancillas and physical execution reported separately. Compare at the same success target and input representation. This is an accounting template, not a proven bound for any proposed algorithm.

## Candidate mechanism comparison

| Candidate and precise promise | Recognition, residual difficulty and counterexample | Classical / quantum baseline and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simon hidden XOR:** `h:{0,1}^n→Y`, nonzero `s`, and `h(x)=h(y)` iff `y=x` or `y=x⊕s`. | The two-to-one oracle promise is supplied. It needs `2^(n−1)` distinct outputs, so for `n>2` a one-bit SAT validity oracle cannot itself satisfy it. A circuit computing `h` may reveal `s` syntactically; the black-box lower bound then need not apply. No reduction from a hard SAT residual to a cheaply built `h` is known here. | Simon recovers `s` with `O(n)` coherent queries; classical randomized black-box collision search needs exponentially many queries. **Control only:** established oracle separation; pivot only if a costed, non-witness-encoding SAT construction is proved. |
| **Shor periodicity:** efficiently computable `g(x)=a^x mod N` with `gcd(a,N)=1`, order `r`, and a Fourier-sampling/continued-fraction recovery condition. For factoring, useful runs also require suitable even `r` and a nontrivial square root of one. | Modular exponentiation is the explicit reversible map; its period is useful for factoring. Ordinary CNF validity has no such period promise. Factoring is not to be labeled NP-complete; an arbitrary SAT encoding of factoring does not make general SAT periodic. | Compare classical number-field-sieve factoring and period methods with Shor's full modular-exponentiation, QFT and repetition cost. **Suspend as SAT transfer; retain as a model for honest oracle construction.** |
| **Hidden subgroup/shift:** a known group action and coset-constant or shifted function `g(x)=h(x⊕s)` with access to the required oracle(s). | The promise is external unless constructible from the CNF. Bent-function shift recovery has known quantum advantages, while a delta-function shift is unique marked search. No meaningful NP-hard SAT reduction with charged oracle construction is established. | Compare specialized classical hidden-shift algorithms with known HSP/hidden-shift quantum algorithms, then Grover if the function is delta-like. **Control only; no V8.** |
| **Boolean Fourier:** a specified, efficiently accessible spectral coefficient or distribution gap of `F(x)=(-1)^f(x)`. | A unique marked `f` has one-sample Walsh probabilities independent of target position (V7 derivation). Explicit affine syntax exposes parity coefficients to classical Gaussian elimination. Estimating a useful coefficient must count phase-oracle and sampling costs. | Bernstein–Vazirani is a black-box parity control; direct affine solving and classical spectral analysis are baselines. **Suspend SAT operator pending a certified, nontrivial spectrum tied to a hard residual.** |
| **Quantum walk/spectral graph:** an explicit reversible Markov chain on partial states, marked mass `ε`, gap `δ`, and costs `S,U,C`. | An incidence/hypergraph spectrum is not automatically the spectrum of the chosen search walk. Building a walk and checking markedness may conceal SAT. A poor gap or tiny `ε` removes the benefit. | MNRS walk cost has the setup/update/check form `S + O((U/√δ+C)/√ε)` under its hypotheses; compare classical walk, CDCL and quantum backtracking with full reversible costs. **Pivot only with a proved walk gap and cheap checking.** |
| **Quantum backtracking:** a reversible classical backtracking tree of `T` nodes with bounded-cost partial-state predicate. | Branching and propagation can reduce `T` classically. Modern CDCL's clause learning is not automatically a reversible tree oracle. | Montanaro gives `O(√T n^(3/2) log n)` tests in its model; compare direct CDCL, DPLL and Grover. **Use as established quantum baseline, not a V8 novelty.** |
| **Affine `Ax=b` plus nonlinear residual `H(x)`:** solve over GF(2), then `x=x₀+Bz` with `d=n−rank(A)` free bits and verifier `H(x₀+Bz)`. | Gaussian elimination and a basis are polynomial to obtain and verify. If `H` is absent, XOR-SAT is in P. If `H` is arbitrary 3-SAT on free variables (add fixed fresh variables if a nonzero rank is desired), satisfiability remains NP-hard by direct reduction. The affine part alone gives no information about which `z` satisfies `H`. | Classical Gaussian elimination plus direct CDCL on residual; standard Grover `O(√(2^d/k))` residual checks for `k` witnesses; quantum backtracking. **Retain as a transparent control, not a new operator.** State-preparation/CNOT and substituted-oracle costs matter. |
| **Quadratic equations over GF(2):** given a system of degree at most two, seek a common zero. | The representation is syntactically recognized, but general MQ remains NP-complete. For a clause `a∨b∨c`, the auxiliary equations `u=(1+a)(1+b)` and `u(1+c)=0` are equisatisfiable; Lean proves their Boolean truth-table equivalence. A polynomial algebraic representation is not an efficient solver. | Compare Gröbner/XL, SAT encodings and CDCL, Grover and quantum backtracking. **Defer:** no efficiently exploitable period, shift or spectrum follows merely from degree two. |
| **Constraint hypergraph:** clauses form hyperedges; a supplied bounded-width decomposition or acyclic join tree organizes dependencies. | Check the decomposition and pay to find it. Bounded width gives classical dynamic programming with exponential dependence on width; if width grows, no tractability claim follows. Disconnected components can be solved classically. A graph's local connectivity score does not certify satisfiability of a half. | Compare width-based DP, CDCL, classical/quantum backtracking and a quantum walk only after its gap is derived. **Pivot to certified width only for a stated family; no operator yet.** |
| **Partial complement:** a nonempty domain `D` and `T` with `f(Tx)=1−f(x)` for `x∈D`, or a single constraint `c` flipped while residual `r` is preserved. | If a point `x∈D` is efficiently produced, one classical `f(x)` value gives a full witness (Lean). If only `c` flips, finding an `r`-witness can remain NP-hard: `F(x,b)=b∧H(x)` with flip of `b` is satisfiable iff `H` is. If `r` is not preserved, even that transfer need not hold. | Compare direct residual SAT, propagation and symmetry reduction. **Reject as an independent quantum search advantage; retain the residual-equivalence theorem for a limitations paper.** |
| **Partition correlation:** a computable statistic `Z(x)` whose conditional means differ by `γ` with a *certified sign that identifies the solution-containing half*. | Such a sign guarantee is a strong promise; empirical branch scores provide none. For bounded `Z`, ordinary sampling estimates the sign with `O(γ^(−2) log(1/η))` samples, while coherent amplitude estimation can use `O(γ^(−1))` oracle calls under its access model. For a one-sided rare existence event, sampling costs `O(1/p)` and amplification `O(1/√p)`. A polynomial gap gives a polynomial classical method too; tiny gaps remain expensive. | Compare classical sampling, direct CDCL, amplitude estimation/amplification and backtracking with all oracle preparation/inverse costs. **Investigate only as a stated promise problem; no certified SAT family or distinct operator yet.** |

The Simon, Shor, hidden-shift, Fourier and walk rows are **existing quantum mechanisms**, not discoveries of this project. Their oracle query advantages do not transfer to explicit NP-hard SAT without a reduction that constructs the required reversible oracles and preserves the intended task.

## What interference actually extracts

Under Simon's exact promise, measuring the output register of the coherent `h` query leaves an input pair proportional to `|x⟩+|x⊕s⟩`. A Walsh transform gives an amplitude factor `1+(−1)^(y·s)`, so measured `y` obeys `y·s=0` over GF(2). Repeated independent equations determine `s`. The **many-output collision oracle** creates this pair state; a Boolean SAT verifier with one marked assignment does not. [Simon](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/S0097539796298637) explicitly states the oracle promise and expected linear number of repetitions.

For [Shor](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9508027), measuring an efficiently computed periodic modular function leaves a coset of exponents `x₀+jr`; the QFT concentrates probability near frequencies representing multiples of `1/r`. Continued fractions and classical checks extract a useful order. An arbitrary CNF phase oracle supplies neither the modular function nor an efficiently meaningful period. For [MNRS walks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0608026), the informative spectral gap is that of the *chosen reversible search transition*, with coherent stationary-state setup and marked checking charged; it is not simply an eigenvalue of a clause-incidence graph.

For a proposed local XOR complement `T(x)=x⊕d` on a union of complete pairs `S`, preparing `|S⟩=|S|^(−1/2)Σ_{x∈S}|x⟩`, applying the **full-validity** phase oracle and then `H^⊗n` gives amplitude `A_y=(N|S|)^(−1/2)Σ_{x∈S}(−1)^{f(x)+x·y}`. Each pair contributes a factor `1−(−1)^(d·y)`, so characters with `d·y=0` cancel. If the relation holds only for one clause, if the domain omits partners, or if the residual verifier changes along a pair, that cancellation does not follow for the full formula. If a point of a certified `S` is efficiently accessible, the local-complement Lean theorem already returns a witness with one classical validity call. If only *quantum* preparation of `|S⟩` is efficient, measuring that state first and then using the classical one-query rule is a necessary baseline; any advantage would arise from access to `S`, not automatically from a new complement phase.

## Lean results and complexity scope

`RCPQI.local_complementing_witness` proves that a supplied local complement law plus a point in its domain yields a witness after one classical validity value. It does not find the point or certify the law. `RCPQI.constraint_complement_residual_witness` proves that a witness of a preserved residual can be converted to one of `c∧r`; it does not find the residual witness. `RCPQI.gated_residual_existence_equivalence` proves `∃(x,b), b∧H(x) ↔ ∃x,H(x)`, making the NP-hard residual reduction explicit at the Boolean level. `RCPQI.clause_quadratic_gadget` proves a local 3-clause/MQ encoding truth table. The NP-hardness and query-lower-bound corollaries rely on standard external complexity results and are **not** Lean theorems. No new axioms, `sorry`, or semantic oracle assumptions were introduced.

For the affine control, the same principle is analytic: polynomial-time elimination gives `x=x₀+Bz`, but setting fresh affine variables to fixed constants leaves any original 3-SAT formula on `z` unchanged. Thus an affine promise may be easy to recognize yet fail to simplify the hard core. For the MQ control, each clause gadget introduces one auxiliary `u`; interpreting Boolean `AND` as multiplication over GF(2) and `NOT a` as `1+a` makes each equation quadratic. Replacing each 3-SAT clause preserves satisfiability with polynomial input growth. These are **restricted-family reductions**, not a proof that all structured systems are hard.

## Operator gate and next direction

No candidate supplies all of: a reproducible recognizer/certificate; a genuinely hard residual family; an information-bearing measurement prediction; a reversible implementation with full `P,S,U,C,R,M,V` costs; and a strict advantage over equally informed direct classical solvers and established quantum methods. Therefore V8 remains **suspended**. Partial complementarity and generic affine/quotient search support a **limitations/equivalence** direction. Simon, Shor and hidden shift remain calibration controls for what a real structural promise looks like.

A scoped limitations paper is plausible after formalizing the uniform oracle-query simulation for quotient search and surveying prior art on local complement and redundant-variable reductions. The present Lean theorems and reductions are rigorous within their statements but elementary; no novelty or publication claim is made. A future positive direction would need a concrete family where a cheaply certified residual statistic or walk gap leads to a proved, costed quantum advantage beyond amplitude amplification/backtracking. Until then, prioritize theorem/counterexample work over additional circuit tuning.
