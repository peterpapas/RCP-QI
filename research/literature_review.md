# Literature review: structural complementarity (2026-11-26)

This targeted review uses original papers and official tool documentation, not a claim that every adjacent algorithm has been surveyed. The cited abstracts or papers were checked against their original publisher, author-hosted, or arXiv pages. Query, gate, and elapsed-time claims use different cost models.

| Area | Primary source and established result | Implication for RCP-QI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unstructured search | [Grover (1996)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605043), [Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani (1997)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001), and [Zalka (1999)](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.2746) establish the generic quadratic search benchmark and black-box limits. | A cheap partition-existence query cannot be assumed for arbitrary black-box `f`. These results do not rule out speedups from stated structure and a stronger input representation. |
| Amplitude amplification | [Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055) amplify the success probability of an algorithm `A`, counting uses of `A` and its inverse; [Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034) handle unknown marked counts. | A constraint-aware state preparation must count preparation, inverse, success marking, verification, and failed runs. Standard amplification of a better classical heuristic is not automatically a new partition mechanism. |
| Quantum backtracking and CSP | [Montanaro (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02374) gives a quantum walk search of a classical backtracking tree with `T` vertices using `O(√T n^(3/2) log n)` tests under its model. [Campbell–Khurana–Montanaro (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05582) cost SAT and graph-coloring implementations against classical algorithms and discuss fault-tolerance overhead. | A branch heuristic can change `T` classically; a quantum claim must beat the corresponding quantum backtracking baseline with coherent branching and predicate costs. |
| Quantum walks for structured search | [Magniez–Nayak–Roland–Santha (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608026) construct marked-state search from a Markov chain, including setup, update, and checking ingredients. | A proposed constraint graph walk needs an efficiently implemented chain, reflection/check operation, and comparison with classical walk/backtracking and known quantum walks. |
| Boolean Fourier/Walsh–Hadamard structure | [Bernstein–Vazirani (1997)](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539796300921) exhibits a promised linear Boolean oracle where Fourier sampling identifies the hidden string. [Gavinsky–Roetteler–Roland (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.3017) relate Boolean hidden-shift complexity to Fourier/influence properties. | Walsh spectra can encode exploitable promises. A generic CNF need not have a sparse, accessible, or informative spectrum; estimating useful coefficients and preparing phase access carry costs. |
| Hidden subgroup and shift | [Mosca–Ekert (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903071) treats Abelian hidden subgroups; [van Dam–Hallgren–Ip (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0211140) gives efficient hidden-shift examples under specific algebraic promises. | A known, efficiently computable group action or shift is a strong promise, not a property of arbitrary SAT. State what oracle supplies the promise and how it is certified. |
| Quantum SAT using problem structure | [Hogg (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9812049) uses SAT conflict structure in a single-step interference algorithm and reports remaining exponential behavior on hard instances. [Brehm–Weggemans (2024/2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13274) benchmark quantum backtracking/Grover against a modern classical SAT solver with structure and fault-tolerant `T` costs. | Clause-specific phase operators and structure-sensitive amplitudes have precedent. Any new operator needs a distinct property and a matched resource comparison. |
| Classical branching and propagation | [Moskewicz et al., Chaff (2001)](https://www.princeton.edu/~chaff/publication/DAC2001v56.pdf) emphasize efficient Boolean constraint propagation and decision heuristics. [Eén–Sörensson, MiniSat (2003)](https://minisat.se/downloads/MiniSat.pdf) combine watched literals, learning, and dynamic ordering. [PySAT solver API](https://pysathq.github.io/docs/html/api/solvers.html) exposes incremental assumption calls and accumulated decisions/conflicts/propagations. | Prefix self-reduction must be compared with a direct modern solver. Probe scoring, clause scans, solver calls, and warm-start effects are part of the classical cost. |
| Symmetry reduction | [Crawford–Ginsberg–Luks–Roy (1996)](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1996-012.pdf) develop symmetry-breaking predicates and discuss symmetry detection. | Variable swaps can reduce equivalent branches, but finding and proving them is preprocessing. A symmetry benefit shared by classical and quantum algorithms cannot be attributed to quantum interference. |
| Tractable controls and phase transition | [Aspvall–Plass–Tarjan (1979)](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2007WS/2SAT.pdf) give a linear-time 2-SAT algorithm. [Kirkpatrick–Selman (1994)](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/selman/papers/pdf/science-94-kirkpatrick-selman.pdf) study the random SAT transition; its location is distribution and finite-size dependent. | XOR-SAT is solvable by Gaussian elimination over `GF(2)` and 2-SAT by implication-graph SCC; they are controls for structural detection, not NP-complete evidence. `m/n≈4.25` is only a generator setting here, not a hardness guarantee. |

## V7 source verification and mechanism gaps

The following claims were checked against the linked original papers on 26 November 2026. They distinguish the authors' oracle problems from SAT search with an explicit CNF input.

| Mechanism | Verified original result | Remaining gap for RCP-QI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantum backtracking | [Montanaro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02374) proves `O(√T n^(3/2) log n)` backtracking-tree tests under the paper's model, including no-solution detection. [Campbell–Khurana–Montanaro](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2019-07-18-167/) evaluate Grover and quantum backtracking for SAT/graph coloring with fault-tolerance assumptions. [Brehm–Weggemans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13274) report that many gains disappear for their structured benchmark and `T`-count model. The latter two assessments have different instance and hardware assumptions; neither is a universal practical verdict. | A transformed recursive method must compare coherent tree tests, update costs and fault-tolerant gates against quantum backtracking, not just full-space Grover. |
| Quantum walks | [Magniez–Nayak–Roland–Santha](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608026) use a Markov-chain walk, approximate reflection and amplification to find marked states. | A constraint-aware walk needs a proven marked fraction/gap plus charged coherent setup, update, check and inverse operations. A graph metaphor alone gives no algorithm. |
| Hidden shifts | [Roetteler](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3208) gives polynomial-time, constant-query quantum hidden-shift algorithms for particular bent functions with exponential classical query separation. [Gavinsky–Roetteler–Roland](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.3017) analyze Boolean hidden shift through Fourier influence and prove an average-case separation. [Childs–Kothari–Ozols–Roetteler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.4642) show that the easiest one-query functions are exactly bent functions while delta functions are hard instances. | These are shift-recovery problems with known/base and shifted-function oracle promises. No costed reduction from an NP-hard CNF family to such a promise has been supplied. A delta-function verifier returns to ordinary unique search. |
| Hidden subgroups | [Mosca–Ekert](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903071) analyze efficient Abelian hidden-subgroup algorithms via eigenvalue estimation under a group-oracle promise. | An arbitrary SAT verifier is not a coset-constant oracle. A proposed group action must state its hiding function, certify the promise, and charge reversible evaluation before an HSP algorithm is a relevant comparator. |
| Symmetry breaking | [Crawford–Ginsberg–Luks–Roy](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1996-012.pdf) show symmetry-breaking predicates can be added to SAT, with polynomial special cases but apparently intractable full generality. | Syntactic generator certification, semantic validity preservation, finding the group, orbit canonicalization and generating full breaking predicates are different tasks. V7 proves a coNP-hardness reduction for the semantic check of one supplied bit flip. |
| Boolean Fourier and feasibility-preserving preparation | [Bernstein–Vazirani](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539796300921) establish a promised Fourier-oracle separation. [Hadfield et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03489) introduce alternating operators that can mix within a feasible subspace under hard constraints. [Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055) give amplitude amplification with uses of preparation and its inverse counted. | Explicit XOR syntax is classically tractable; a feasibility mixer or prepared subspace does not reveal a witness by itself. The cost of deriving, preparing and reflecting about the subspace must be lower than the residual search benefit. |

The [V7 mathematical review](v7_mathematical_review.md) derives a one-classical-query complement witness rule, a quotient factorization equivalence, a semantic-symmetry certification reduction and a unique-marker Walsh counterexample. They are original derivations within this project **under their stated assumptions**, not claims of priority over the wider literature. No new circuit passes the operator gate.

## Research questions left open

1. Can a transformation `T` and its relation to a clause-built verifier be discovered and certified in polynomial time on a **specified** family, with a useful partition statistic not already available to an equally informed classical solver?
2. Can a reversible constraint-aware preparation or walk update use that statistic while keeping all setup, uncomputation, ancillas, precision, and repetitions below matched quantum backtracking and amplitude-amplification costs?
3. What certificate distinguishes a structural promise that is easy to recognize from one that merely restates the hard existence question?
4. For a family with genuine automorphisms, does symmetry reduction change query complexity after charging group discovery and orbit representation? Compare direct CDCL, symmetry-aware CDCL, quantum backtracking, and any proposed operator.

No source above establishes a polynomial-time quantum algorithm for unrestricted NP-complete SAT. The V1–V3/V6 experiments establish narrower negative results for the tested circuits and inputs only.

## V7.1: promises versus explicit SAT structure

The [V7.1 structural promise taxonomy](v7_1_structural_promise_discovery.md) checks the following original sources and records each promise, discovery obligation, residual problem and matched baseline. [Simon's original paper](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/S0097539796298637) explicitly requires a many-output two-to-one XOR oracle or a one-to-one alternative; its expected `O(n)` oracle repetitions and classical oracle separation do not apply to a one-bit SAT verifier. [Shor's original factoring/discrete-log paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9508027) uses efficiently computed modular arithmetic and period recovery, not a generic SAT clause oracle. [Mosca–Ekert](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903071) require an Abelian hidden-subgroup promise, and [Childs–Kothari–Ozols–Roetteler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.4642) show that hidden-shift query complexity depends sharply on the base function: bent functions and delta functions behave differently.

For structured search, [Magniez–Nayak–Roland–Santha](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0608026) include coherent setup, update, marked-state checking and spectral gap in their walk bound, while [Montanaro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02374) bounds backtracking-tree tests under its model. [Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055) already give amplitude amplification and estimation, so an improved sampling or branch statistic is not itself a novel quantum operator.

For explicit constraints, [Schaefer's original classification](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350) separates tractable Boolean constraint languages from NP-complete ones. [Courtois–Klimov–Patarin–Shamir](https://www.nicolascourtois.com/papers/xlfull.pdf) state that general quadratic systems over GF(2) remain NP-complete and discuss classical algebraic solvers. [Gottlob–Leone–Scarcello](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1999/57) distinguish tractability from restricted constraint relations and from hypergraph decomposition; [Allender et al.](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v010a012/v010a012.pdf) document width-parameterized SAT algorithms with exponential dependence on width. [Hadfield et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03489) provide precedent for feasibility-preserving quantum operators, whose state-preparation and mixer costs must still be charged.

These sources do not supply a reduction from general or NP-hard SAT to Simon, Shor, hidden-shift or walk promises. The V7.1 Lean theorems instead establish scoped counterexamples for local complementarity and algebraic representation. No V8 operator was approved.
