# Literature review: structural complementarity (2026-11-26)

This targeted review uses original papers and official tool documentation, not a claim that every adjacent algorithm has been surveyed. The cited abstracts or papers were checked against their original publisher, author-hosted, or arXiv pages. Query, gate, and elapsed-time claims use different cost models.

| Area | Primary source and established result | Implication for RCP-QI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unstructured search | [Grover (1996)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605043), [Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani (1997)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001), and [Zalka (1999)](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.2746) establish the generic quadratic search benchmark and black-box limits. | A cheap partition-existence query cannot be assumed for arbitrary black-box `f`. These results do not rule out speedups from stated structure and a stronger input representation. |
| Amplitude amplification | [Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055) amplify the success probability of an algorithm `A`, counting uses of `A` and its inverse; [Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034) handle unknown marked counts. | A constraint-aware state preparation must count preparation, inverse, success marking, verification, and failed runs. Standard amplification of a better classical heuristic is not automatically a new partition mechanism. |
| Quantum backtracking and CSP | [Montanaro (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02374) gives a quantum walk search of a classical backtracking tree with `T` vertices using `O(√T n^(3/2) log n)` tests under its model. [Campbell–Khurana–Montanaro (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05582) cost SAT and graph-coloring implementations against classical algorithms and discuss fault-tolerance overhead. | A branch heuristic can change `T` classically; a quantum claim must beat the corresponding quantum backtracking baseline with coherent branching and predicate costs. |
| Quantum walks for structured search | [Magniez–Nayak–Roland–Santha (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608026) construct marked-state search from a Markov chain, including setup, update, and checking ingredients. | A proposed constraint graph walk needs an efficiently implemented chain, reflection/check operation, and comparison with classical walk/backtracking and known quantum walks. |
| Boolean Fourier/Walsh–Hadamard structure | [Bernstein–Vazirani (1997)](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539796300921) exhibits a promised linear Boolean oracle where Fourier sampling identifies the hidden string. [Gavinsky–Roetteler–Roland (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.3017) relate Boolean hidden-shift complexity to Fourier/influence properties. | Walsh spectra can encode exploitable promises. A generic CNF need not have a sparse, accessible, or informative spectrum; estimating useful coefficients and preparing phase access carry costs. |
| Hidden subgroup and shift | [Mosca–Ekert (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903071) treats Abelian hidden subgroups; [van Dam–Hallgren–Ip (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0211140) gives efficient hidden-shift examples under specific algebraic promises. | A known, efficiently computable group action or shift is a strong promise, not a property of arbitrary SAT. State what oracle supplies the promise and how it is certified. |
| Quantum SAT using problem structure | [Hogg (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9812049) uses SAT conflict structure in a single-step interference algorithm and reports remaining exponential behavior on hard instances. [Brehm–Weggemans (2024/2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13274) benchmark quantum backtracking/Grover against a modern classical SAT solver with structure and fault-tolerant `T` costs. | Clause-specific phase operators and structure-sensitive amplitudes have precedent. Any new operator needs a distinct property and a matched resource comparison. |
| Classical branching and propagation | [Moskewicz et al., Chaff (2001)](https://www.princeton.edu/~chaff/publication/DAC2001v56.pdf) emphasize efficient Boolean constraint propagation and decision heuristics. [Eén–Sörensson, MiniSat (2003)](https://minisat.se/downloads/MiniSat.pdf) combine watched literals, learning, and dynamic ordering. [PySAT solver API](https://pysathq.github.io/docs/html/api/solvers.html) exposes incremental assumption calls and accumulated decisions/conflicts/propagations. | Prefix self-reduction must be compared with a direct modern solver. Probe scoring, clause scans, solver calls, and warm-start effects are part of the classical cost. |
| Symmetry reduction | [Crawford–Ginsberg–Luks–Roy (1996)](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1996-012.pdf) develop symmetry-breaking predicates and discuss symmetry detection. | Variable swaps can reduce equivalent branches, but finding and proving them is preprocessing. A symmetry benefit shared by classical and quantum algorithms cannot be attributed to quantum interference. |
| Tractable controls and phase transition | [Aspvall–Plass–Tarjan (1979)](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2007WS/2SAT.pdf) give a linear-time 2-SAT algorithm. [Kirkpatrick–Selman (1994)](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/selman/papers/pdf/science-94-kirkpatrick-selman.pdf) study the random SAT transition; its location is distribution and finite-size dependent. | XOR-SAT is solvable by Gaussian elimination over `GF(2)` and 2-SAT by implication-graph SCC; they are controls for structural detection, not NP-complete evidence. `m/n≈4.25` is only a generator setting here, not a hardness guarantee. |

## Research questions left open

1. Can a transformation `T` and its relation to a clause-built verifier be discovered and certified in polynomial time on a **specified** family, with a useful partition statistic not already available to an equally informed classical solver?
2. Can a reversible constraint-aware preparation or walk update use that statistic while keeping all setup, uncomputation, ancillas, precision, and repetitions below matched quantum backtracking and amplitude-amplification costs?
3. What certificate distinguishes a structural promise that is easy to recognize from one that merely restates the hard existence question?
4. For a family with genuine automorphisms, does symmetry reduction change query complexity after charging group discovery and orbit representation? Compare direct CDCL, symmetry-aware CDCL, quantum backtracking, and any proposed operator.

No source above establishes a polynomial-time quantum algorithm for unrestricted NP-complete SAT. The V1–V3/V6 experiments establish narrower negative results for the tested circuits and inputs only.
