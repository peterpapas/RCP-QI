# Literature review — initial primary sources

This is a starting bibliography, not a novelty search. Any new operator or complexity claim requires a targeted literature review.

- Grover, [*A fast quantum mechanical algorithm for database search*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605043) (1996): generic amplitude amplification baseline for one marked item.
- Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard, Vazirani, [*Strengths and Weaknesses of Quantum Computing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001) (1997): black-box search lower-bound context.
- Boyer, Brassard, Høyer, Tapp, [*Tight bounds on quantum searching*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034) (1998): search when the number of marked items is not fixed.
- Zalka, [*Grover's quantum searching algorithm is optimal*](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.2746) (1999): optimality of generic quantum search.
- Brassard, Høyer, Mosca, Tapp, [*Quantum Amplitude Amplification and Estimation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055) (2002): framework relevant to partition-existence estimation.

The current randomized unknown-count schedule is a small experimental heuristic, not an implementation or proof of the full Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp algorithm. SAT self-reduction, structured quantum SAT methods, and modern classical solvers still need a focused review before novelty can be assessed.
